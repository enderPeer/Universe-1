## What was built

- `sim/soup.py`: normative Python reference of a Tierra/Coreworld-style soup on the crawler ISA (LDIND, STIND, INCM, JNZ, W = 5),
  segment-relative addressing so the exp06c copiers are valid verbatim; processors conserved; birth by a watcher (8 words written by
  this processor, this processor the last writer of each, equal to its birth genome); oldest-dies reaper; death by age; placement of
  free processors at random positions, with or without 8 random words of inflow code; birth mutation; rays; Bedau neutral shadow.
- `life/u1soup.cu`: CUDA engine, bit-identical to the reference on ten configurations (`fast/check_soup.py` compares final memory,
  every processor, counters, every statistics row, censuses, activity files). 2^20 words with 65,536 processors run at 2,000 to 2,700
  ticks/s on the RTX 4090/4080, 750 to 1,400 on an RTX 3060. CUDA only: no Vulkan port, so the AMD nodes did not take part.
- `cluster/run_soup.py` + `cluster/soup_runs.conf`: dispatch to the five NVIDIA cards (detached on the nodes, resumable).
  `cluster/analyze_soup.py`: chains of births, census and Bedau statistics, plots.

## What was measured before the rules were fixed (docs/12, section 1)

Every one of the 503 listed exp06c copiers has soup fecundity exactly one: a copy loop's pointers are its own instruction words and
a 3-bit operand survives eight increments. The three "intact" replicators need the exp06c code+1 pre-fill and never copy in a zero
or random soup. NANO (Dimension42) is the same: 314 of its 316 replicators have fecundity one, two have two. A content-blind birth
rule fires on a shifted junk copy before the real one; without the last-writer condition parents are credited with their children's
copies (Fibonacci-like phantom growth).

## What the ensemble showed (16 soups, about 60 million ticks)

- Random soup with processor inflow only: cools into inert fixed points within 1,000 ticks and stops sampling program space.
- Random-code inflow, dense (one processor per 16 words): copiers every 20,000 to 80,000 ticks, but every word is rewritten about
  once per tick and copies die before their processor attaches; no chain beyond 2.
- Random-code inflow, sparse (one processor per 512 words) with a 128-step processor life: 128 births in 46 chains, the longest 34,
  20, 12 and 8 generations, each a single genome copied faithfully by its own instructions; 16 children altered by neighbours' writes,
  one of which copied itself once more. At 8,192 processors: 233 births, 138 chains, longest 26.
- Seeded soups (4,096 of the 462 robust copiers; mu 0.002 / 0.01 / 0.05): about 840 births in the first few hundred ticks, then none.
  Closed soup (no inflow): extinct at tick 288. The fecundity-two copier alone: 1,163 births, no persistent line.
- No genome ever outran the neutral shadow; no lineage grew. Verdict: transient novelty, bounded reproduction, no open-ended
  evolution, for a structural reason (fecundity one).

## Next actions (for whoever takes them)

   sparse random-code soup at W = 6 is the direct test of whether fecundity two unlocks growth. Needs `a = 6` (reach 64) and the
   genome in 48 bits (fits the u64 genome).
2. Vulkan port of `life/u1soup.cu` for falke64 and specht32 (same structure as `life/u1life.comp`).
3. Longer sparse soups with several seeds, and a census interval short enough (a few ticks) for the activity statistics to see a
   chain; or activity accumulated from births.tsv instead of censuses.
