Where this starts from:

  every world reaches a flat equilibrium; in the JC worlds the winner at 200 k ticks is still the winner at 4.5 M ticks with three
  times the share. Closed system by construction (`results/life/OVERNIGHT_RESULTS.md`).
- Audit (`docs/11_implicit_and_explicit.md`): 21 unstated, consequential assumptions; five ranked tests, three of them under an hour.
- Design C soup (`docs/12_design_c_soup.md`, `results/soup/ANALYSIS.md`, other session): engine and reference identical; random
  soups freeze within 1,000 ticks unless the inflow brings random code; with random-code inflow a copier appears every few ten
  thousand ticks and starts a chain of at most 20 generations; seeded soups make one generation of children and stop; no genome
  outruns the neutral shadow. Cause, predicted in docs/12 before the run: fecundity is one by construction, because the copy loop's
  pointers are part of the 8-word genome and are worn out by the copy.

AMD cards and the CPUs are idle; the workstation holds all Life frames (about 85 GB, gitignored); knecht24's disk is at 88 %.

## 2. Tomorrow: design C2, the machine change that lets fecundity exceed one

Pick one of the three options in docs/12 section 7, ranked here by how little it adds to the world:
1. **Sampled W = 6 crawler sweep** (4-bit operands, pointers survive 16 increments): the existing `gpu/u1_l2` kernel with `--W 6`,
   2^40 random programs of the 2^48 space on the nine cards (a few GPU-hours), copy-only, scoring fecundity (how many distinct
   non-overlapping copies the program finishes within 256 steps). If fecundity-two or -three copiers exist, seed a soup with them
   and we stay inside "nothing chosen but the word width".
2. **Pointer registers** (Tierra's answer): a processor carries source and destination pointers outside the genome; new ISA, new
   2^32 sweep for copiers (one GPU-hour), then the soup. More expressive, but the machine is no longer the crawler.
3. **Variable length** via a length-aware watcher or a divide instruction: the only route to the "unbounded complexity" half of
   open-endedness; it is a new rule and should come after 1 or 2 shows a sustained lineage.
Recommendation: 1 first (cheap, uses existing kernels, keeps the design honest), 2 if 1 finds nothing, 3 once anything persists.
Measurement stays as built: births per generation, lineage depth from `births.tsv`, Bedau activity against the shadow, novelty of
birth genomes; add length and reach distributions when 3 arrives.
